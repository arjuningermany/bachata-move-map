The 8 patterns, mapped

Diagrams show the shape: where the leader and follower start, travel, and land, plus the count and the one lead cue that matters.

What a still can't show is the lead and timing of a moving partner. Two moves below are flagged see it moving, treat the picture there as a map, not a lesson.

How to read these

  • Gold disc, the leader
  • Teal ring, the follower
  • Dashed arrow, travel path across the floor
  • Curved arrow, a turn / rotation on the spot
  • L is gold, R is teal, always · solid = weight on it · hollow = tap / no weight
01

The basic

count 1234 · 5678 4 & 8 = hip pop

Stay soft in the knees the whole time, the hip pop on 4 and 8 is a result of bending and straightening the knees, not something forced from the hips.

The same rhythm gives two variations: step nearly in place to hold the spot, or step forward-and-back to travel, swapping between all three is most of a song.

02

Cross-body lead

timing8-count, prep, travel across, resolve · the glue between everything

The leader opens the lane and pivots out of the way, giving a clear forward lead so the follower walks straight across the slot. Both dancers end facing each other again, spots swapped.

03

Follower's right turn

timingled over a 4-count (e.g. prep on 4, turn 5-6-7, land on 8)

The leader raises the left hand into a loose ceiling and preps a beat early, so the turn is expected. The follower spot-turns underneath. Guide the arm, never crank it.

04

Follower's left turn

timingsame 4-count feel, turning the other way

Prep in the opposite direction first, then indicate the turn clearly, the left turn feels less natural to a follower, so the signal has to be a touch stronger and earlier.

05

Closed ↔ open

timingover any basic, this is how the "room" changes

The leader's right hand sits on the follower's shoulder blade, left hand out to the side, bodies close and slightly offset. Lead in to close the frame, and open back up to send the follower out to arm's length.

From closed, raising the left hand leads a simple underarm turn: a clean, high-value move that costs nothing new.

06

Armlock / sombrero

timinga turn with a wrapped exit

Lead a turn and, instead of releasing, let the arm wrap: either folding behind the small of the follower's back, or both hands arcing up and over the heads like putting on a hat.

see it moving, the entry is all in the timing
07

Shadow position

timingled out of a turn that doesn't resolve to face

Lead a turn but keep the follower turned away instead of resolving to face. The follower ends directly in front, both dancers facing the same direction, with the frame over the follower's shoulders.

08

Body roll / wave

timingon a pause or a slow phrase, yours alone, no partner

Initiate at the chest, let the movement roll down through the ribs, stomach and hips in one continuous wave. This is styling, and it is what reads as dancing.

see it moving, drill it slow in the mirror first

Master these eight and any song is danceable. Everything else is decoration on top.
